Temperature ±0.5°C and humidity from one sensor. Monitors the 40–65°C thermophilic zone and 50–70% humidity window.

MQ-135 Gas

Detects ammonia and CO₂ spikes. 48-hour warmup guard prevents false alerts. Active after sensor stabilisation.

Moisture Probe

Capacitive sensor measures compost pile wetness without corrosion. Maps raw ADC to 0–100% scale. Ideal: 50–60%.

FAQ

Frequently Asked
Questions.

No. BioBot runs entirely on solar power. The 5.5V panel charges an 18650 battery via TP4056. A 1N5819 Schottky diode prevents reverse drain at night. All sensors, motor, display, and WiFi run from stored solar energy.
BioBot uses three active sensors: DHT22 for temperature and humidity, MQ-135 for ammonia and CO₂ gas detection, and a capacitive moisture probe for pile wetness. The DS3231 RTC tracks the exact calendar day count stored in NVS flash memory so it survives power cuts.
The ESP32 pushes sensor data to Blynk every 5 minutes via WiFi. You see live gauges for temperature, humidity, gas, moisture, day count, and health status. Alerts fire when any sensor goes out of range. You can also trigger the motor manually via the Mix button (V6, Push mode).
A full cycle takes 45–60 days through four stages: Mesophilic (Days 1–7), Thermophilic (Days 8–20) with peak heat 55–65°C, Cooling (Days 21–45), and Curing (Days 46–60). BioBot notifies you when compost is ready to harvest.
Yes, fully. Without WiFi, BioBot continues reading all sensors, auto-mixing every 8 hours, counting days via the DS3231 RTC, and displaying live readings on the OLED. Only Blynk monitoring and the Telegram AI bot require internet.
The Telegram bot runs independently on Railway cloud — completely separate from the ESP32. It uses Qwen 3.6 27B AI to answer composting questions in Tamil and English. It works 24/7 even when BioBot is powered off.
